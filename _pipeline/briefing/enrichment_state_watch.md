**Division of labor is the entire purpose of this block.** The top-level `body` carries
the *national/structural* analysis; State Watch is where it becomes *local and
actionable for the reader's actual markets*. Obey these rules or the callouts just
restate the body (the exact failure mode this design exists to prevent):

- **Focus on the specific markets the reader follows — nothing else:**

__FOCUS_MARKETS__

  Speak to *those* markets. Do **not** drift to other metros in the state except as a
  brief contrast that sharpens the read on a followed market. A callout that stops at
  the state level, or names a market not listed above, has missed the point.
- **If the story doesn't reach a followed market, say so briefly** rather than
  substituting a different one — e.g. "the flagged counties are elsewhere in the state —
  no read-through to the reader's market." That is more useful than a generic state take.
- **Never infer a followed market's conditions from a nearby or "comparable" market
  instead of real data on that market.** "St. John, closer to Chicago's exurbs, should be
  underwritten more like Cook County" is exactly the failure this bans — a guess by
  proximity, stated as a finding, with nothing about St. John itself behind it. If you
  found no data specific to the followed market, that itself is the callout: say plainly
  that you found no local data on this point, rather than substituting an analogy dressed
  up as a fact.
- **No overlap with the body.** No fact, figure, or place name may appear in both. If
  the body cites a county or a number, the State Watch on that state must go one level
  deeper (the followed market, or a local detail the body didn't give) **or** switch
  dimension (financing → insurance → property tax → exit liquidity). Every callout must
  add something the reader did not already get from the body.
- **End each callout with the move**, not a restatement: the concrete action in *that
  market* — at acquisition, refinance, or underwriting (e.g. "model a tighter ~70% DSCR
  LTV on that book", "the refi window there is open", "price the insurance step-up before
  the next deal").
- **Cover every focus state, or say why not.** Prefer one line per followed market. If a
  state's market sees only national-baseline impact, keep a short one-clause note saying
  so rather than dropping it silently. Omit a state entirely only when the story does not
  touch it at all.
- **Keep the top-level body at national altitude.** Write `body` as the
  structural/national thesis — mechanism, drivers, what it means for a small SFH
  investor in general. States may appear only as *illustrations* of the trend; do not
  enumerate per-state specifics or roster the followed markets in the body. Litmus test:
  if you deleted the State Watch, the body should still read as a complete national
  analysis, and it should contain zero facts the State Watch repeats.
